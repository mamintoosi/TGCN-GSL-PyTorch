\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

We evaluate whether learning the adjacency from traffic data improves
forecasting relative to a physical road-network graph, and under what
conditions. Unless noted otherwise, each value is the mean test RMSE over
five training seeds with the sample standard deviation in parentheses;
learned graphs are fitted once and shared across seeds. Improvements are
relative RMSE reductions with respect to the named reference. Statistical
qualifications follow Section~\ref{sec:stats}.

\subsection{Baselines: Physical Graph versus Graph-Free Control}\label{sec:res-physical}

Table~\ref{tab:tgcn-main} reports the T-GCN family on both benchmarks. The
physical road-network adjacency is the weakest configuration in its family
in every dataset--horizon cell. On Los-loop at PH1, T-GCN obtains
$7.88\pm 0.28$ RMSE, while the graph-free control T-GCN-NoSpatial obtains
$5.25\pm 0.19$ --- a $33.3\%$ relative reduction when the physical graph is
removed. The same direction holds at PH2--PH4 on Los-loop ($29.2\%$,
$27.0\%$, $24.1\%$) and at every horizon on SZ-Taxi ($24.4\%$--$25.2\%$).
NoSpatial beats Physical in $5/5$ seeds in all eight cells.

The GCN family (Table~\ref{tab:gcn-main}) shows the same pattern
(Los-loop PH1: GCN $8.14\pm 0.11$ vs.\ GCN-NoSpatial $4.88\pm 0.31$).

These baselines matter for evaluating GSL: an improvement over the physical
graph alone does not establish that learned structure is useful, because
removing the graph already reduces error substantially. Figure~\ref{fig:graphstruct}
contrasts the dense physical adjacency with the much sparser multi-lag
learned union on Los-loop.

\subsection{Contemporaneous Learned Graphs (GSL and cGSL)}\label{sec:res-single}

Replacing the physical adjacency with a single contemporaneous DAGMA graph
(Section~\ref{sec:gsl_contemp}) improves on Physical in most T-GCN cells but
does not surpass the graph-free control.

On Los-loop PH1, T-GCN-GSL and T-GCN-cGSL obtain $5.86\pm 0.21$ and
$5.82\pm 0.23$, about $26\%$ better than Physical yet about $11\%$ worse
than NoSpatial. The ordering Physical $>$ single learned graph $>$ NoSpatial
(in error) holds at all Los-loop horizons and on SZ-Taxi: neither
contemporaneous learned graph beats the graph-free control in any
dataset$\times$horizon cell.

For GCN, cGSL is clearly better than the directed GSL on Los-loop
($5.76\pm 0.20$ vs.\ $7.83\pm 0.11$ at PH1) but still worse than
GCN-NoSpatial. For T-GCN, GSL and cGSL are nearly indistinguishable.
Symmetrization therefore matters mainly for the non-recurrent backbone that
applies a single symmetric-style aggregation; this is an empirical
observation, not a general rule.

In short, a single learned contemporaneous graph recovers only part of the
error reduction obtained by discarding the physical adjacency.

\subsection{Is the Gain Only Sparsity?}\label{sec:res-controls}

Table~\ref{tab:controls} reports the matched-sparsity control on Los-loop
PH1. At a common budget of $30$ directed edges, a random graph (RandTop30)
obtains $6.10\pm 0.12$ and a top-correlation graph (CorrTop30) obtains
$5.39\pm 0.10$ --- both worse than NoSpatial ($5.25\pm 0.19$). The DAGMA
multi-lag graphs at the same budget obtain $4.84\pm 0.11$ under fixed
per-step use and $4.49\pm 0.14$ under gated use
(Section~\ref{sec:res-multilag}). Thus, on this cell, sparsity alone does
not explain the multi-lag gain; where the edges are placed matters. The
conclusion is limited to Los-loop PH1.

A capacity-matched graph-free model (hidden $74$; single seed; lower block
of Table~\ref{tab:controls}) stays at about $5.14$ RMSE, so the extra
parameters of the gated variant do not by themselves account for its error
level.

\subsection{Multi-Lag Learned Graphs}\label{sec:res-multilag}

The multi-lag construction estimates lag-specific graphs
(Section~\ref{sec:gsl_multilag}). On Los-loop these graphs are informative
($12$/$3$/$15$ edges by lag; union $28$). Consuming them with a fixed
lag-aligned assignment in T-GCN (T-GCN-MultiGSL) yields $4.84\pm 0.11$ at
PH1: better than NoSpatial by $7.8\%$ and better than Physical by
$38.5\%$. Per-node gating (T-GCN-MultiGSL-Mix) improves further to
$4.49\pm 0.14$.

Relative to the graph-free control, Mix reduces RMSE by $14.5\%$, $11.9\%$,
$9.2\%$, and $10.9\%$ at PH1--PH4 on Los-loop (Table~\ref{tab:tgcn-main};
$5/5$ paired seeds at every horizon). Relative to the physical T-GCN
baseline, the reductions are $43.0\%$, $37.6\%$, $33.7\%$, and $32.3\%$.
Figure~\ref{fig:perseed} shows the per-seed distributions: Mix is the
strongest Los-loop configuration at every horizon. The global-weight
variant (Weighted) is essentially tied with the fixed assignment
(differences $\le 0.01$ RMSE).\label{sec:res-mix}

When the \emph{same} multi-lag artifacts are merged into one static union
and consumed by non-recurrent GCN (GCN-MultiGSL), error is $9.78\pm 0.14$
on Los-loop PH1 --- worse than the physical GCN and more than twice the
T-GCN-MultiGSL error on the same graphs (Figure~\ref{fig:dissociation}).
The union retains $28$ of the $30$ lagged edge slots. Hence the edge set
alone does not determine usefulness: how the learned multi-lag structure is
used inside the backbone is a major factor. Because GCN and T-GCN also
differ architecturally, this is not a fully isolated test of consumption,
but it shows that learned multi-lag graphs are not automatically beneficial
once estimated.
\label{sec:res-dissociation}

\subsection{Dataset Dependence and Temporal Resolution}\label{sec:res-boundaries}

\paragraph{SZ-Taxi.}
On SZ-Taxi the multi-lag fit retains only two lagged edges. All learned
variants remain close to the graph-free control: Mix vs.\ NoSpatial differs
by at most $0.015$ RMSE (relative reductions $\le 0.34\%$), with Mix winning
$4/5$, $4/5$, $5/5$, and $4/5$ paired seeds at PH1--PH4. The strong
Los-loop multi-lag result therefore does not transfer to SZ-Taxi under the
present construction.

\paragraph{Los-loop at 15-minute sampling (resolution control).}
SZ-Taxi is sampled every $15$ minutes, whereas Los-loop is sampled every
$5$ minutes. To test whether the weak SZ-Taxi separation could be an
artifact of coarser temporal resolution rather than of the dataset itself,
we resampled Los-loop to $15$-minute steps by averaging consecutive triplets
(Section~\ref{sec:variant15}) and reran the multi-lag comparison on that
series.

At $15$ minutes ahead, NoSpatial obtains $8.600\pm 0.249$,
MultiGSL $7.246\pm 0.298$, and Mix $6.240\pm 0.187$ --- a $27.4\%$ relative
reduction vs.\ NoSpatial with $5/5$ seed wins, \emph{larger} than the
$14.5\%$ obtained at $5$-minute sampling. Relative reductions remain
$21.4\%$, $19.8\%$, and $16.1\%$ at $30$, $45$, and $60$ minutes
(Table~\ref{tab:los15}). Absolute RMSE values are not comparable across
sampling intervals, and PH indices of the variant are not those of the
$5$-minute series.

Because Los-loop at $15$-minute sampling improves \emph{more} than at
$5$-minute sampling, while SZ-Taxi at the same $15$-minute interval stays
near the graph-free baseline, temporal resolution alone does not explain
the SZ-Taxi result. The contrast instead points to dataset-specific
factors, including how much lag structure the estimator recovers
($30$ lagged slots on $5$-minute Los-loop, $32$ on $15$-minute Los-loop,
versus only $2$ on SZ-Taxi).

\subsection{Summary of Findings}\label{sec:res-summary}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Learning $\mathbf{A}$ from data (GSL) improves on the physical graph
in many settings, but a graph-free control is necessary to judge the true
contribution of spatial structure.
\item A single contemporaneous learned graph does not beat the graph-free
control on these benchmarks.
\item Multi-lag learned graphs help on Los-loop when used in a lag-aligned
way in T-GCN; gated mixing is the strongest configuration. Sparsity alone
does not explain this gain on the matched-budget cell.
\item The benefit is dataset-dependent: SZ-Taxi shows little separation
from the graph-free baseline, consistent with a nearly empty multi-lag fit.
\end{enumerate}
