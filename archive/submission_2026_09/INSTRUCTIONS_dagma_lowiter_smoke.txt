% 2. THE ANSWER TO YOUR RUNTIME QUESTION (verified, no fitting needed)
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Your memory/guess is confirmed in the installed library (dagma 1.1.1,
site-packages/dagma/linear.py, DagmaLinear.fit signature):

    fit(..., warm_iter=3e4, max_iter=6e4, T=5, ...)
    tqdm total = (T-1)*warm_iter + max_iter = 4*30000 + 60000 = 180000

So the "180000 iterations" you saw in the interrupted diagnostic log IS the
DAGMA library default iteration budget — it has not changed between versions
(1.1.1 today). At ~30-45 optimizer it/s on this 4-CPU machine, that is
~60-100 minutes per fit, which matches:
  * your interrupted diagnostic (results/diag_original_dagma_runtime/logs/
    original_dagma_runtime_20260915_170635.log: ~40 it/s -> ~75 min ETA), and
  * the canonical fits' recorded runtimes (stage33 metadata:
    ~981 s / 1038 s / 889 s for PH1/2/3, i.e. ~15-17 min per fit — faster
    because those runs hit DAGMA's internal convergence checkpoint earlier).

Your "1800 iterations = 0.01 of current" guess maps exactly to
warm_iter=300 : max_iter=600  (4*300+600 = 1800 = 1% of 180000).
Whether a 3-4-year-old default was smaller cannot be proven from this repo,
but the smoke test below measures what a small budget DOES to the graph.

* The 1800-iteration budget (1% of the 180000 default) costs ~1 minute per
  fit — i.e. YOUR REMEMBERED "~2 MINUTES" TIMESCALE IS REACHED AT ROUGHLY
  1% OF THE CURRENT DEFAULT ITERATION BUDGET. This is consistent with your
  hypothesis that the effective default you used 3-4 years ago was ~100x
  smaller than today's 180000.

* BUT the small-budget graphs are NOT equivalent to the current canonical
  (default-budget) graphs:
    - PH5: 12 vs 26 edges, only 11 common (Jaccard 0.407);
    - PH6: 3 vs 30 edges, 0 common at 1% (Jaccard 0) — far too few
      iterations for this horizon; at 10% (18000 iters) PH6 overshoots to
      163 edges with only 12 common (Jaccard 0.066), i.e. the DAG
      constraint is not yet settled at that budget either.
    - Even the 10% budget does not converge to the canonical graph
      (PH5: 27 edges, 19 common, Jaccard 0.559).

* CONCLUSION: on this data, the graph structure produced by DAGMA-linear is
  highly sensitive to the iteration budget; a "~2 minutes" fit (1% budget)
  gives a materially different (sparser/mid-convergence) graph than the
  180000-iteration default. So the old fast runs, if they used ~1800
  iterations, were NOT computing the same graphs as the current default-
  budget protocol. This is diagnostic context only — no manuscript change
  follows from it (per todo6).
