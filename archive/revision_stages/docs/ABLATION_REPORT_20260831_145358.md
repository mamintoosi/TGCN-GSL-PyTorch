# Sparsified-Physical-Graph Ablation Report

**Date:** 2026-08-31 14:53:58  
**Repository:** TGCN-GSL-PyTorch  
**Paper:** "Graph Structure Learning for Traffic Prediction"  
**Purpose:** Determine whether the forecasting improvement from GSL/cGSL comes from *learned topology* or simply from *graph sparsification* (reduced oversmoothing)

---

## 1. Executive Summary

1. **64 experiments completed** — 2 datasets × 2 models × 4 horizons × 4 graph types, all successful.
2. **The key experiment**: replace DAGMA-learned graphs with *random* graphs of the same edge count. If the random graph performs similarly to GSL, the improvement comes from sparsification, not topology.
3. **SZ-Taxi / TGCN: Sparse-random OUTPERFORMS GSL** (110–123% coverage). The random sparse graph is better than the DAGMA-learned graph. This is the most damaging finding for the paper's claims.
4. **SZ-Taxi / GCN: Both contribute.** Sparse-random covers ~63% of the GSL improvement. GSL topology provides an additional ~37% beyond sparsification.
5. **Los-loop / TGCN: Sparsification dominates.** Sparse-random covers ~79% of the GSL improvement. GSL provides ~21% additional benefit.
6. **Los-loop / GCN: Sparse-random is harmful.** Random sparsification destroys GCN performance on Los-loop (+23% RMSE). But GSL improvement over physical is tiny (0.8–2.3%).
7. **cGSL is always the best performer** — symmetrization consistently helps, especially on Los-loop.
8. **The paper's claims about "learned topology" are not fully supported** — for 3 of 4 dataset/model combinations, sparsification is the dominant factor.
9. **The reviewer concern is valid**: a substantial portion of the reported gains IS attributable to reduced oversmoothing from sparsity.

---

## 2. Objective

Reviewer 1 raised a critical concern:

> "The learned graphs are likely much sparser than the physical graph. Please report the density/degree distribution and check whether some of the reported gains are attributable to reduced oversmoothing from sparsity rather than the specific learned structure."

This experiment directly addresses this by introducing a **sparse random graph baseline**: a random graph with the *exact same number of edges* as the GSL graph, but with random edge placement. This isolates the effect of edge count from the effect of edge identity.

## 3. Experimental Design

### Graph Types

| Type | Code | Description | Edges (SZ) | Edges (LA) |
|------|------|-------------|-----------:|----------:|

| GSL | `use_gsl=1` | DAGMA-learned directed graph | 8 | 28–39 |
| cGSL | `use_gsl=2` | DAGMA-learned, symmetrized (A + A^T) | 16 | 56–78 |

### Sparse Random Graph Construction

For each dataset and prediction horizon:
1. Count edges in the GSL graph (e.g., 8 for SZ-Taxi).
2. Sample that many random (i, j) pairs from the N×N matrix (excluding diagonal).
3. Set those entries to 1 in a symmetric binary matrix.
4. Save as `data/sparse_random_{dataset}_pre_len{ph}.npy`.

### Configuration

- **Models:** GCN (hidden_dim=100), TGCN (hidden_dim=100)
- **Epochs:** 50
- **Batch size:** 64
- **Learning rate:** 0.001
- **Random seed:** 42 (fixed for all experiments)
- **Sequence length:** 12
- **Split ratio:** 0.8 train / 0.2 validation
- **Device:** NVIDIA RTX 3090 (CUDA)

## 10. Root Cause Analysis

### Why does sparse random outperform GSL for SZ-Taxi / TGCN?

The GSL graph has only **8 directed edges** connecting **9 nodes** (out of 156). With TGCN's recurrent architecture, the graph structure determines how information propagates across time steps. The DAGMA-learned edges may connect nodes that are statistically correlated but not temporally useful for prediction, while a random graph with 8 edges has a chance of including more useful connections. The extreme sparsity (94% isolated nodes) means that for TGCN, *which* 8 edges are chosen matters less than *having* 8 edges (vs 532).

### Why is sparse random harmful for Los-loop / GCN?

Los-loop has 207 nodes with 2833 physical edges (density 0.066). Reducing to 28 edges (density 0.00065) is a 101× reduction. For GCN (which aggregates neighbor features), this extreme sparsification removes almost all spatial information. The physical graph's connectivity is essential for GCN to function. However, DAGMA's learned 28 edges are *better placed* than random, providing a tiny improvement.

### Why does cGSL always win?

cGSL symmetrizes the GSL graph (A + A^T), doubling the edge count (16 for SZ, 56–78 for LA). This provides more spatial information while still being much sparser than the physical graph. The symmetrization also ensures bidirectional information flow, which is more natural for traffic networks.

## 11. Implications for Reviewer 1

### What this experiment establishes

1. **For 3 of 4 dataset/model combinations, sparsification is the dominant factor** (63–123% of the improvement).
2. **For SZ-Taxi / TGCN, the DAGMA topology is actually harmful** — a random graph performs better.
3. **The GSL improvement for Los-loop / GCN is marginal** (0.8–2.3% RMSE reduction).
4. **cGSL consistently provides the largest improvement**, suggesting symmetrization is the key contribution, not DAGMA specifically.

### What this experiment does NOT establish

1. Whether a *different* sparse graph (e.g., top-K correlation, spatial proximity) would outperform both GSL and random.
2. Whether the DAGMA-learned edges are meaningful for interpretability.
3. Whether a lambda/threshold sweep would produce denser, more informative graphs.

### What additional experiment is needed

A **top-K correlation baseline**: compute pairwise Pearson correlation between nodes, keep the top-K edges (matching GSL edge count), and compare. This would test whether DAGMA provides *any* benefit beyond what simple correlation would give.

## 12. Implications for Reviewer 2

### Interpretability claims

The paper claims the learned graph reveals "hidden causal structure" and "temporal propagation patterns." This ablation shows:

- For SZ-Taxi / TGCN: the specific edge identities don't matter (random is better).
- The 8-edge graph connecting 9 nodes out of 156 cannot meaningfully represent "causal structure" in a 156-node traffic network.
- The "interpretability" of the learned graph is questionable when a random graph performs equally well or better.

### Visualization claims

The paper's graph visualizations (Section 5) show a small subgraph of ~9 nodes. While visually appealing, this subgraph represents only 5.8% of the network and may not be representative.
